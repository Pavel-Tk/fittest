05 · Survival of the fittest

Evolution beats static, once the gate tests forces.

pre-registered · 12 new seeds each · scored on 2,475 never-read frames (new formulas)

Breed → gate on forces → prune what adds nothing → serve the best answer.

Δ force RMSE vs static, eV/Å · filled = seed significant · ◆ mean ± 95% t-CI · results/evolution_f/FRESH_FRAMES.md

Talking points, in order. We pre-registered what would count as evolution working. Runs A, B and C failed that test, and run D, a pre-registered fix that protected base models and softened the gate, was not confirmed (4 of 6 seeds, 5 needed). The diagnosis: the gate's reaction-energy check, computed on a handful of labels, did not predict held-out results (r = −0.15) and mostly rejected the children with the largest force gains. Run F switched that check off, keeping breeding, the force gate and fitness pruning, and was pre-registered on 12 new seeds: served force error fell versus the static swarm in all 12 (−0.026 eV/Å, 95% interval −0.030 to −0.021 on the eval split). Run E, also pre-registered, grew the swarm without any selection and gained −0.018. Because the fix came from reading earlier eval results, we pre-registered a second check before scoring: 2,475 frames from new formulas that no run had ever read. The eval numbers reproduced exactly, and the gains held: run F −0.026, 12 of 12 seeds better; run E −0.018, 12 of 12. The original gate on run E's seeds is the contrast: −0.010, with 4 seeds worse than static. A sham control, children with learning rate zero, gains only −0.004 (not significant), so the force gain comes from learning. Not everything passed: the CPU replication of run E failed the fresh-frame rule with 2 seeds worse. The live service still runs the static swarm.
