Talking points: we pre-registered what would count as evolution working, and it has not cleared that bar. Run A bred MACE children: 15 of 18 were stillborn, mostly on reaction energy, and in seed 1 the child that replaced base MACE improved forces but made reaction energies worse. Run B bred AIMNet2 children with a reaction-energy loss: it holds in one seed of two. Run C bred niche specialists from all three families at three DFT budgets: one seed holds at 200 and at 800, none at 400. The rule needs every seed, so the verdict is no. Most children fail the gate on reaction energy (36 of 54 in run C, 15 of 18 in run A). Why evolved swarms sometimes end up worse than static is not yet established; one hypothesis is that selection prunes base models for niche specialists, but no-selection changes several things at once and lost to evolved in one of the two pre-registered seeds, so we do not claim it. The pre-registered secondary outcome: the evolved swarm has lower reaction-energy error than static in 9 of 10 seed runs, but children are trained and gated on reaction energy, and fine-tuning without selection gains as much. The static swarm on slide 05 is the result we stand on.
